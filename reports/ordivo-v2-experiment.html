Ordivo v2

Can a shared decision model learn from different kinds of real choices and rankings while leaving arithmetic, hard constraints and unknown preferences to a verifiable decision engine?

What the experiment must distinguish

A formula can rank alternatives exactly when its method, inputs and weights are specified. A neural model can predict a person's observed or stated choice. Those are different claims. Ordivo v2 asks whether MCDM training helps a shared model predict held-out real decisions beyond a matched behavioral-only model and strong specialist baselines.

Mathematical answer

Exact COPRAS, WASPAS, VIKOR and TOPSIS results are conditional on declared criteria, weights and candidate set. A verifier recomputes numerical claims and feasibility.

Behavioral prediction

Click, booking, stated choice, relevance and explicit ranking remain distinct labels. A good score on one does not certify another.

Decision support

Unknown weights remain unknown. The report must show alternative outcomes under plausible weight scenarios, hard-constraint results and remaining uncertainty.

What is measured today?

Current v2 result

Three official real-data sources have grouped, four-way remote builds. Swissmetro and ESCI have held-out classical baselines. A shared two-domain neural pilot learned on its selected validation groups, but a bounded paired held-out probe scored lower than base d1 on Swissmetro top-1 accuracy (0.66 versus 0.68, 450 scenarios) and ESCI NDCG@10 (0.7523 versus 0.7783, 27 of 50 selected groups; 23 overflowed). Swissmetro NLL improved while Brier worsened. Two matched C1/R0 continuations then worsened selection-validation loss versus their shared start. Expedia has a source build, not a benchmark. ESCI classical scores depend measurably on the tie convention; no superiority follows. None of this establishes a public v2 release or cross-domain generality.

Preliminary paired neural probe

Validation learning did not translate into better held-out top-line scores

The shared Swissmetro/ESCI adapter completed 40 optimizer steps and reduced selected-validation loss on both sources. A later bounded, paired held-out probe compared the frozen base and adapted checkpoint on the same selected units. The adapted model scored lower on Swissmetro top-1 accuracy and ESCI NDCG@10. Swissmetro NLL improved while Brier worsened. This is exploratory evidence, not a protected final release test or a statistical superiority claim.

Historical v1 result, not a v2 benchmark

The saved v1 synthetic pilot improved single-winner agreement from 25/87 to 51/87 eligible test episodes. Its ten-bin ECE moved from 0.11830 to 0.23938, while Brier error improved from 0.78108 to 0.62310. These small-sample results concern imitation of exact synthetic teachers. The test split was reused for diagnostics, so v2 needs a newly protected final test. Read the source audit.

A fixture or passing unit test proves an implementation path, not generalization. A metric without an event definition, split hash and denominator is omitted.

How a fair comparison works

1 · AcquireRecord source terms, file hashes, feature meanings and complete source groups.
2 · FreezeSplit by search, query or respondent into disjoint training, model-selection, calibration and final-test roles.
3 · CompareRun specialist baselines, base d1, frozen v1, behavioral-only and MCDM-pretrained variants on matched groups.
4 · VerifyMeasure ranking, proper probability scores, constraint outcomes, robustness and actual costs.

Expedia booking/click observations need exposure analysis. ESCI and MSLR grades measure relevance within source-sampled judged candidate pools, not retrieval recall across every product or page; unjudged does not mean irrelevant. Swissmetro choices are stated. SUSHI provides explicit rankings. Entire-domain holdouts test whether one shared model transfers; separate models on separate datasets do not answer that question. A public shared-model generality claim needs at least three real domains covering choice and relevance plus actual held-out-domain evidence. A two-domain baseline remains preliminary. Three matched seeds and paired group-level intervals are required for final neural comparisons.

Where uncertainty remains

Preference evidence

The same option set can support different rational rankings under different weights. Neither entropy weights nor a neural guess establish a person's priorities.

Observational bias

Shown position affects clicks. Expedia's randomized-order flag is useful for a slice, but alone does not give causal preference estimates or known propensities.

Generalization

Large candidate sets, missing features, languages and domain shifts can break performance. Failed or truncated groups must stay in coverage accounting.

What would justify a release?

The public package needs a frozen model and data record, rights review, matched held-out comparison, failure analysis, calibration and verifier evidence, reload demonstration and cost accounting. Every gate currently awaiting a qualifying v2 receipt remains pending.

  1. Confirm access and model distribution rights for each source; publish hashes and terms records without raw restricted rows.
  2. Freeze complete-group splits and feature allowlists, then run domain-appropriate baselines and the shared neural comparisons.
  3. Evaluate final groups once under the declared rules; report all exclusions, failures and intervals.
  4. Verify base-plus-adapter loading, reproducibility, notices, sanitized report and public model/data cards before any announcement.
